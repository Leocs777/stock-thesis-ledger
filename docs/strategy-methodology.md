# Strategy methodology

Status: decision-v4.1.1, Stock Thesis Ledger v0.2.1.

This document describes the implemented deterministic rules in `app.py`. It is
a reproducibility specification, not evidence that the rules predict returns.
The engine is not trained, fitted, or generated by machine learning.

## Inputs and eligibility

A current decision requires at least 60 cached daily OHLCV bars. The factor
calculator requires 51; the stricter decision gate leaves enough history for
the 60-session drawdown window. The engine also checks freshness, OHLC
consistency, calendar gaps, depth, and split-scale discontinuities. A blocker
or data older than seven calendar days produces `Refresh data`, not an
actionable classification.

Inputs are:

- Alpha Vantage cached end-of-day OHLCV;
- cached SEC EDGAR facts and their filing dates, when available;
- the active strategy style or immutable custom template;
- Day, Swing, or Long-term horizon; and
- local Paper position exposure and the saved maximum-position limit.

When `INVESTORLAB_ADJUSTED_DAILY=1`, the Alpha Vantage adjusted-close factor is
applied to each historical open, high, low, and close before those values enter
research calculations. Volume remains the provider's reported raw volume.
Adjustment coverage is stored by symbol and date; the data-quality response
states whether the active window is historically adjusted. Without full
coverage, the engine retains raw prices and warns about split/dividend risk.

Identical input contexts reuse the prior stored result through a configuration
hash. Every stored result records model version, factor scores, evidence,
counter-evidence, data quality, invalidation, and the exact strategy version.

## Technical evidence

The raw technical card totals 85 points before it is rescaled into the active
strategy's technical allocation.

| Factor | Raw maximum | Implemented rule |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Trend | 35 | 18 when close > SMA20, plus 17 when SMA20 > SMA50 |
| 20-session momentum | 20 | 20 at >= 5%; 14 above 0%; 7 above -5%; otherwise 0 |
| 60-session close drawdown | 10 | 10 at >= -5%; 7 at >= -10%; 3 at >= -20%; otherwise 0 |
| Annualized daily volatility | 10 | 10 at <= 20%; 7 at <= 35%; 3 at <= 55%; otherwise 0 |
| Latest volume / 20-session average | 10 | 10 at >= 120%; 6 at >= 80%; otherwise 3 |

Volatility uses the sample standard deviation of daily close returns multiplied
by `sqrt(252)`. Drawdown uses closes, not intraday lows. Strategy-specific
technical mixes redistribute the chosen technical allocation across these five
factors, then each raw score is scaled into its assigned maximum.

## Fundamental evidence

SEC facts are converted into transparent 0-100 raw factor cards before strategy
weighting:

Original and amended SEC facts are retained by filing date. A historical run
reconstructs each fiscal period from the newest fact vintage that was public on
that date; the current research view shows only the latest vintage per period.

- **Growth:** average of available revenue and net-income growth. Each receives
  100 at >= 15%, 80 at >= 8%, 60 at >= 3%, 40 at >= 0%, otherwise 0.
- **Profitability:** net margin receives 100 at >= 20%, 80 at >= 10%, 60 at
  >= 5%, 40 at >= 0%, otherwise 0.
- **Free-cash-flow quality:** FCF/revenue receives 100 at >= 15%, 80 at >= 8%,
  60 at >= 0%, otherwise 0.
- **Balance sheet:** liabilities/assets receives 100 at <= 40%, 75 at <= 60%,
  40 at <= 75%, otherwise 0.
- **Valuation:** close / positive diluted EPS receives 100 at <= 15x, 75 at
  <= 25x, 45 at <= 40x, 20 for a higher positive ratio, otherwise 0.
- **Income:** dividends per share / close receives 100 at >= 4%, 80 at >= 2.5%,
  50 at >= 1%, 25 above 0%, otherwise 0.

These are hand-authored thresholds. They do not estimate intrinsic value and do
not normalize for industry, accounting policy, fiscal seasonality, or macro
regime.

## Strategy and horizon weights

Default allocations sum to 100 because the remaining 15 points are reserved for
Paper position fit.

| Style | Technical | Fundamental | Position fit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Balanced | 60 | 25 | 15 |
| Growth | 40 | 45 | 15 |
| Value | 35 | 50 | 15 |
| Income | 30 | 55 | 15 |
| Momentum | 75 | 10 | 15 |

Each style also has a distinct internal technical and fundamental mix. Day
shifts up to 10 points from fundamentals to technical evidence. Long-term shifts
10 points from technical to fundamentals. Swing keeps the base allocation.
Immutable custom templates instead preserve their saved technical,
fundamental, valuation, portfolio, and per-side cost weights.

### Missing fundamentals

The behavior is explicit rather than silently imputed:

- Balanced and Momentum defaults move their full configured fundamental weight
  to technical evidence when no SEC metrics are cached.
- Growth, Value, and Income defaults retain their fundamental allocation;
  unavailable raw factors score zero and the decision is marked partial.
- A custom template moves missing fundamental **and valuation** weight to
  technical evidence. Its saved portfolio weight does not move.
- Partial individual factor coverage is reported as a percentage. Missing
  factors score zero; available factors are not inflated to hide the gap.

This fallback is a product rule, not an empirical claim. Validation reports
should segment runs with and without complete fundamentals.

## Position fit and classifications

Position fit is 15 for no current position. For a held Paper position it is:

- 15 at or below 75% of the saved maximum-position percentage;
- 10 at or below 100% of that limit;
- 5 at or below 125%; and
- 0 above 125%.

Default strategies use those raw 0-15 points directly. A custom template scales
the same raw fit into its saved portfolio allocation.

For an unheld symbol, a score of 75-100 is `Buy candidate`, 55-74 is
`Watch / wait`, and below 55 is `Avoid for now`. For a held Paper position,
a close below SMA50 or score below 45 is `Sell / exit review`; score below 60
or position fit below 10 is `Reduce exposure`; otherwise it is `Hold`.

These labels organize review. They are not orders or personalized investment
recommendations.

## ATR price scenarios

Price levels are separate from the 0-100 score and never increase it. The engine
uses a 14-session average true range, where true range is the maximum of
high-low, |high-previous close|, and |low-previous close|.

- Pullback zone: `min(close, max(SMA20, close - ATR))` through the latest close.
- Breakout trigger: `max(close, prior 20-session high) + 0.1 ATR`.
- Stop candidate: the greater of SMA50 and one ATR below the pullback-zone low,
  then capped at least 0.25 ATR below the reference close.
- Per-share risk: reference close minus the resulting stop.
- Target 1: reference close plus saved minimum reward/risk times per-share risk.
- Target 2: reference close plus one additional R beyond Target 1.

Targets are inactive for Avoid, Reduce, and Sell / exit review states. All
levels use cached end-of-day bars and are hypothetical, not forecasts or
executable quotes.

## Portfolio and option accounting

Equity and option positions are keyed separately by `(symbol, asset_type)` so a
stock lot and an option contract cannot be merged. Values use:

`quantity × price × asset multiplier`, where the multiplier is 1 for equity and
100 for options. Realized P/L uses the same multiplier. Equity positions use the
latest cached daily close when available; option positions fall back to their
paper average cost because the daily-equity cache is not an option mark feed.
The UI labels that fallback. Gross-position weight uses total invested exposure,
while account weight uses the saved paper account size. Industry exposure is a
descriptive grouping from cached SEC company metadata.

The standard option worksheet calculates expiration intrinsic value for every
leg, subtracts the entered premium, applies side, quantity, and the 100-share
multiplier, then sums the legs. The flexible scenario samples 25 underlying
prices. Its pre-expiration line retains quoted extrinsic value using a square-root
time-decay heuristic and a user-supplied IV shift. Its delta and theta are
simplified scenario measures, not Black-Scholes prices or executable Greeks.

## Day-trade evidence and sizing

The risk worksheet computes per-share risk from entry-to-stop distance. The
maximum whole shares are the strictest of the per-trade risk budget, remaining
daily-loss capacity, and saved maximum-position allocation. A plan is blocked
when direction geometry, reward/risk, halt state, market phase, spread, volume,
or saved loss guardrails fail their explicit checks.

Intraday VWAP uses typical price `(high + low + close) / 3`, weighted by volume,
from 09:30 through 16:00 America/New_York only. Relative volume divides current
regular-session volume through the latest observed minute by the average volume
through the same minute in available prior sessions. Premarket bars are retained
for premarket levels but do not enter VWAP or relative-volume totals. IEX data is
not consolidated SIP coverage, so every result exposes its feed, timestamp, and
scope.

## Walk-forward scenario

At least 70 daily bars are required. Starting after 50 bars, each step:

1. computes factors using only bars available through that close;
2. selects only SEC fundamentals whose `filed` date is on or before that
   close;
3. requests a long-or-cash state at score >= 75;
4. remains invested while score >= 55; and
5. executes a changed state at the following session close, then applies later
   close-to-close returns only while invested.

The simulation uses neutral 15-point position fit, fractional exposure, and
forces an open state closed at the final cached bar. It reports return, exposure,
maximum drawdown, completed trades, win rate, MAE/MFE, holding duration, and an
equity curve.

The same-symbol buy-and-hold return starts at the walk-forward sample boundary.
If aligned SPY bars are cached, the report also shows descriptive return
relative to SPY. SPY is a comparison series, not a risk-adjusted benchmark or
proof of skill.

### Frozen chronological holdout

When the walk-forward evaluation contains at least 200 scored sessions, the
first 70% form the development segment and the final 30% form a chronological
holdout of at least 60 sessions. The holdout reports its own dates, session count, strategy return,
buy-and-hold return, and aligned SPY comparison only when the complete current
context was recorded on or before the holdout boundary. That context is the
model version, `full-context-v1` freeze protocol, style, horizon, and immutable
strategy version/configuration hash. The freeze date is the first decision in
the latest uninterrupted run of that exact context; switching context ends the
run. A new or resumed context must collect future bars before the final segment
can be called out-of-sample.

Strategy comparison never selects an automatic leader from holdout returns.
Doing so would consume the holdout for model selection. The UI presents each
eligible version separately with its sample size and warning.

The freeze-date check does not create independent evidence if a user repeatedly
inspects and tunes against the final 30%. Once the holdout has influenced a
parameter choice, it is development data and a new future window is required.

### Execution costs

The default base cost is 10 basis points **per entry and exit side**. Each event
adds:

- a range surcharge of `round(daily range bps / 40)`, capped at 25 bps; and
- a dollar-volume surcharge of 20 bps below $1 million, 10 below $10 million,
  5 below $50 million, otherwise 0.

Total modeled cost is capped at 500 bps per side and excludes tax. This is a
conservative heuristic, not a broker fill model.

Per-trade returns and outcomes include both the entry and exit cost events.
Completed-trade counts remain visible at every sample size, but win rate is not
displayed until at least 10 trades are complete.

### Parameter sensitivity

The same point-in-time sample is rerun with:

| Variant | Entry | Exit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Conservative | 80 | 60 |
| Base | 75 | 55 |
| Permissive | 70 | 50 |

The return spread is labeled stable at <= 10 percentage points, mixed at <= 25,
and unstable above 25. Reusing one short cached sample is not out-of-sample
validation; it is only a fragility check.

## Non-ML boundary and limitations

decision-v4.1.1 is deterministic rules code. It has no fitted coefficients,
training set, optimizer, learned ranking, language-model input, sentiment model,
or automatic parameter search. The academic sources below explain why trend,
momentum, valuation, and profitability are reasonable research dimensions; the
implementation is not a reproduction of those papers and inherits none of
their reported performance.

Other limitations include a small locally cached universe, at most 320 daily
bars per decision query, no survivorship-bias correction, optional rather than
universal adjusted-price coverage, raw volume in adjusted mode, next-session-
close execution without intraday fills or partial fills, no tax, and incomplete industry normalization. Review
[the Paper validation protocol](paper-validation-protocol.md)
before making any performance statement.
